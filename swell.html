STATE II — SWELL BUILDING

Crests peak. Troughs round out.

Stack enough sine waves and you get chop, but real swell needs one more ingredient. Each point of water doesn't just bob up and down — it traces a small circle, moving forward at the crest and backward in the trough. That horizontal component is what turns a smooth sine curve into the sharp-crested, broad-troughed shape you're scrolling through, and it's why long, slow-looking swells always outrun the short chop riding on top of them.

STATE IV — ROUGH

Press the surface. Watch it ring outward.

Underneath the swell sits a second simulation: a grid where every cell's height depends only on its four neighbours a moment ago. That's the wave equation, discretised — the same mathematics governing the big rolling swell above, just running at a smaller scale and a faster clock. Move your cursor across the water and you're injecting energy directly into that grid.

READING 03 — COALESCENCE

Nothing here is really separate.

These aren't sprites merging on cue. Each is a real Matter.js body with a soft pairwise pull standing in for surface tension, rendered through a blur-then-threshold filter that turns any two overlapping circles into one continuous blob. Drag two droplets close and watch them stop being two.
